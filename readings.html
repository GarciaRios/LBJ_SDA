Readings

Fall 2026 required and recommended readings

Readings are listed by class session. Complete the Required readings before class. Recommended readings are optional references for students who want additional depth or need them for the semester project.

Access

  • Open access links lead directly to material available without a subscription.
  • UT Libraries links lead to the publisher or DOI record. Sign in through UT Libraries when prompted. A course copy will also be posted on Canvas when permitted.
  • Page ranges refer to the editions listed below. You are responsible only for the chapters, pages, or named sections specified.

Core books

  • Groves, Robert M., Floyd J. Fowler Jr., Mick P. Couper, James M. Lepkowski, Eleanor Singer, and Roger Tourangeau. 2009. Survey Methodology. 2nd ed. Wiley. Book information. UT Libraries/Canvas.
  • Tourangeau, Roger, Lance J. Rips, and Kenneth Rasinski. 2000. The Psychology of Survey Response. Cambridge University Press. Book information. UT Libraries/Canvas.

Students are not expected to read either book cover to cover.

Session 1 · Polls, evidence, and error

Required

  • American Association for Public Opinion Research (AAPOR). 2025. “2024 Pre-Election Polling: An Evaluation of the 2024 General Election Polls,” Executive Summary. Read the complete executive summary. Open access.
  • AAPOR. “Polling Accuracy.” Read the complete webpage. Open access.
  • Groves et al. 2009. Chapter 2, “Inference and Error in Surveys,” pp. 39–64. UT Libraries/Canvas.

Recommended

  • AAPOR. 2025. Full report on 2024 pre-election polling. Open access.

Session 2 · Research objectives, ethics, and total survey error

Required

  • AAPOR. “Code of Professional Ethics and Practices.” Read sections I–III: Principles of Professional Responsibility, Principles of Professional Practice in the Conduct of Our Work, and Standards for Disclosure. Open access.
  • AAPOR. “Best Practices for Survey Research.” Read the complete webpage. Open access.
  • Groves et al. 2009. Re-read Chapter 2, pp. 48–64, focusing on Figure 2.5 and the individual components of total survey error. UT Libraries/Canvas.

Recommended

  • AAPOR. “Disclosure Standards.” Open access.

Session 3 · Concepts, measurement, and questionnaire architecture

Required

  • Groves et al. 2009. Chapter 7, “Questions and Answers in Surveys,” pp. 217–253. Focus on the relationship among constructs, measurements, responses, and response error. UT Libraries/Canvas.
  • Krosnick, Jon A., and Stanley Presser. 2010. “Question and Questionnaire Design.” In Handbook of Survey Research, 2nd ed., pp. 263–313. Read pp. 263–286, through the end of the section on open versus closed questions. Open-access resource page; course PDF on Canvas.
  • Pew Research Center. “Writing Survey Questions.” Read the sections “Question development,” “Measuring change over time,” and “Open- and closed-ended questions.” Open access.

Recommended

  • Tourangeau, Rips, and Rasinski. 2000. Chapter 1, “The Psychology of Survey Response,” pp. 1–22. UT Libraries/Canvas.
  • Converse, Jean M., and Stanley Presser. 1986. Survey Questions: Handcrafting the Standardized Questionnaire, Chapter 2, “The Logic of Survey Questions,” pp. 9–30. UT Libraries.

Session 4 · Wording, response options, order, and sensitive questions

Required

  • Schwarz, Norbert. 1999. “Self-Reports: How the Questions Shape the Answers.” American Psychologist 54(2): 93–105. Read the complete article. UT Libraries.
  • Tourangeau, Roger, and Ting Yan. 2007. “Sensitive Questions in Surveys.” Psychological Bulletin 133(5): 859–883. Read pp. 859–870 and the conclusion on pp. 878–879. UT Libraries.
  • Pew Research Center. 2018. “Methods 101: Survey Question Wording.” Watch the video and review the examples on the page. Open access.

Recommended

  • Pew Research Center. 2019. “When Online Survey Respondents Only ‘Select Some That Apply.’” Open access.

Session 5 · Cognitive testing and multilingual equivalence

Required

  • Willis, Gordon B. 2005. Cognitive Interviewing: A Tool for Improving Questionnaire Design. Chapter 1, “An Introduction to Cognitive Interviewing,” pp. 1–15, and Chapter 4, “The Practice of Cognitive Interviewing,” pp. 42–65. UT Libraries/Canvas.
  • Survey Research Center. 2016. Guidelines for Best Practice in Cross-Cultural Surveys. Read the chapter “Pretesting: Cognitive Interviewing” and its guidelines, rationale, and procedural steps. Open access.
  • Survey Research Center. 2016. “Translation: Overview.” Read Guidelines 1–4 and their rationales. Open access.

Recommended

  • Pan, Yuling, and Mandy Sha. 2019. The Sociolinguistics of Survey Translation, Chapter 2, “A Sociolinguistic Perspective on Survey Question Design and Translation,” pp. 21–39. UT Libraries.

Session 6 · Sampling and representation

Required

  • Groves et al. 2009. Chapter 3, “Target Populations, Sampling Frames, and Coverage Error,” pp. 69–94. UT Libraries/Canvas.
  • Groves et al. 2009. Chapter 4, “Sample Design and Sampling Error,” pp. 97–118 only, stopping before “Systematic Sampling.” UT Libraries/Canvas.
  • Pew Research Center. 2023. “Comparing Two Types of Online Survey Samples.” Read the overview and “How the probability and opt-in samples compare.” Open access.

Recommended

  • AAPOR. 2013. “Report of the Task Force on Non-Probability Sampling.” Open the report from the AAPOR reports page and read the executive summary. Open access.
  • Kennedy, Courtney, et al. 2016. “Evaluating Online Nonprobability Surveys.” Open access.

Session 7 · Modes, recruitment, coverage, and nonresponse

Required

  • Groves et al. 2009. Chapter 5, “Methods of Data Collection,” pp. 149–177. UT Libraries/Canvas.
  • Groves, Robert M., and Emilia Peytcheva. 2008. “The Impact of Nonresponse Rates on Nonresponse Bias.” Public Opinion Quarterly 72(2): 167–189. Read pp. 167–181 and the conclusion on pp. 186–187. UT Libraries.
  • Pew Research Center. 2015. “From Telephone to the Web: The Challenge of Mode-of-Interview Effects in Public Opinion Polls.” Read the overview and “Mode differences by question content.” Open access.

Recommended

  • AAPOR. 2019. “Transitions from Telephone Surveys to Self-Administered and Mixed-Mode Surveys.” Open the report from the AAPOR reports page and read the executive summary. Open access.

Session 8 · Programming, mobile design, randomization, and QA

Required

  • U.S. Census Bureau. 2026. Design Guidelines for U.S. Census Bureau Web Surveys and Censuses. Read “Overall Look and Feel,” “Screen Elements,” and the entries for single-select, multi-select, grids, and open-text questions under “Question Components.” Open access.
  • Martin, Elizabeth, et al. 2007. “Guidelines for Designing Questionnaires for Administration in Different Modes.” Read the introduction and Guidelines 1–12. Open access.
  • Course instrument QA protocol. Read the complete checklist posted on Canvas and bring it to class. Canvas.

Recommended

  • AAPOR. “Disclosure Standards.” Revisit as a design-stage audit tool. Open access.

Session 9 · Fieldwork, paradata, fraud, and respondent quality

Required

  • AAPOR. 2022. Data Quality Metrics for Online Samples: Considerations for Study Design and Analysis. Read Section 1, “Introduction and Scope,” pp. 1–2, and Section 6, “Summary and Recommendations,” pp. 49–51. Open access.
  • Kennedy, Courtney, et al. 2020. Assessing the Risks to Online Polls From Bogus Respondents. Read the overview, pp. 2–8, and the report’s concluding recommendations. Open access.
  • AAPOR. 2020. “Falsification in Surveys.” Open the report from the AAPOR reports page and read the executive summary and recommendations. Open access.

Recommended

  • Pew Research Center. 2020. “Answers That Did Not Match the Question Were Concentrated in Opt-In Polls.” Open access.

Session 10 · Cleaning, coding, weighting, and documentation

Required

  • Groves et al. 2009. Chapter 10, “Postcollection Processing of Survey Data,” pp. 343–365. UT Libraries/Canvas.
  • Pew Research Center. 2018. “For Weighting Online Opt-In Samples, What Matters Most?” Read the overview and conclusion. Open access.
  • Pew Research Center. 2018. “How Different Weighting Methods Work.” Read the complete methods explainer. Open access.

Recommended

  • Lumley, Thomas. 2010. Complex Surveys: A Guide to Analysis Using R, Chapter 1, “Basic Tools,” pp. 1–14. UT Libraries/Canvas.

Session 11 · Estimation, uncertainty, and subgroup analysis

Required

  • Pew Research Center. 2021. “Why Pew Research Center Will Display Margins of Error in Some Graphics.” Read the complete explainer. Open access.
  • Pew Research Center. 2018. “Variability of Survey Estimates.” Read the complete methods explainer. Open access.
  • Wasserstein, Ronald L., Allen L. Schirm, and Nicole A. Lazar. 2019. “Moving to a World Beyond ‘p < 0.05.’” The American Statistician 73(sup1): 1–19. Read pp. 1–10. Open access.

Recommended

  • Lumley, Thomas. 2010. Complex Surveys, Chapter 2, “Simple and Stratified Sampling,” pp. 15–31. UT Libraries/Canvas.

Session 12 · Visualization and public interpretation

Required

  • Healy, Kieran. 2018. Data Visualization: A Practical Introduction. Read Chapter 1, “Look at Data,” and Chapter 2, “Get Started.” Open access.
  • Schwabish, Jonathan A. 2021. Better Data Visualizations. Read Chapter 2, “Five Guidelines for Better Data Visualizations,” pp. 17–42. UT Libraries/Canvas.
  • AAPOR. “Disclosure Standards.” Read sections 1–3 and use them to assess the assigned polling release. Open access.
  • Applied comparison: The two polling releases named on Canvas. Read the complete toplines and methodology statements before class. Canvas.

Recommended

  • Cairo, Alberto. 2019. How Charts Lie, Chapter 2, “How Charts Lie: Poor Design,” pp. 23–49. UT Libraries/Canvas.

Session 13 · Reporting, peer review, and partner briefing

Required

  • AAPOR. “Transparency Initiative.” Read “What must be disclosed?” and the disclosure elements. Open access.
  • Pew Research Center. “Writing Survey Questions.” Re-read “Measuring change over time” and review how the page documents question wording and order. Open access.
  • Course report style guide and reproducibility checklist. Read both documents in full and apply them to the team draft. Canvas.

No additional long reading is assigned so teams can concentrate on the complete report draft.

Session 14 · Presentation and retrospective

There is no new reading. Review the final report, peer feedback, decision log, and your individual contribution record before class.
